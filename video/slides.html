ASR · homework 1

The four kernel groups we wrote

GLM-ASR-Nano: about 2.3B float32 parameters (9 GB). Audio is padded to 30 s, so the encoder always runs 32 layers over 1500 positions; the 28-layer decoder is greedy, with no KV cache.

Task 1 · RoPE

One thread rotates one pair of numbers. One launch covers both q and k, halving the launch cost over 396 calls. Neighbouring threads touch neighbouring addresses.

Task 2 · Reductions

A warp sums 32 values in 5 shuffle steps; a block combines its warp results in shared memory. Empty slots in a max hold −∞, not 0. Softmax keeps a running (max, sum), so it reads each row twice, not three times.

Task 3 · Tiled GEMM

16×16 tiles of x and W in shared memory; each value is used 16 times. The W tile is loaded with k along threadIdx.x (coalesced) and padded to 16×17 (no bank conflicts). Bias and GELU are fused into the store.

Task 4 · Attention

FlashAttention-style: the score matrix is never stored. 4 warps (4 queries) share each tile of 32 keys and values. Each warp keeps a running max, sum and output, rescaled when a larger score appears. GQA and causal mask.

make test: all 33 cases pass against a CPU reference (GEMM speed also compared with cuBLAS).

Why the GEMM sets the speed

  • Encoder q_proj: ours 1,939 GFLOP/s, naive 234, cuBLAS 13,195
  • Each multiply-add needs two shared-memory reads, so shared memory, not arithmetic, is the limit
  • cuBLAS keeps blocks of values in registers and reuses them (register tiling)
  • Our RoPE and RMSNorm are one launch each; PyTorch builds them from many small operations

RAG · our change

A reranking stage between search and prompt

  1. Exact search returns the top N posts instead of 5 (N = 10, 20, 50, 100)
  2. A cross-encoder scores each (question, post) pair on the GPU: one transformer pass per post
  3. Stable sort by score, keep the top 5 (ties keep enc_a’s order)
  4. Same prompt, same k = 5: only the choice and order of the 5 posts change

Models: ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2 (22M, web-search data) and bge-reranker-base (278M, general data). Batches of 32, at most 512 tokens per pair.

# rag/src/rag.py, answer_transcript()
if self.reranker is None:      # baseline: the original path

    record_ids = retrieval["record_ids"]

        query_vector, k=self.candidate_depth)

        transcript, retrieval["record_ids"], self.data, k)

# rag/extensions/reranker.py, rerank()

# stable sort: ties keep the dense order

RAG · analysis

Why reranking does not help here

Better evidence does give better answers

  • No relevant post in the top 5: judge 0.12 (52 questions), against 0.45 (73 questions)
  • bge: the 17 questions whose Recall@5 rose gained 0.18 on average

… but the rerankers rarely improve the evidence

  • Relevant post at rank 1: 47 questions in the baseline; MiniLM keeps 33, bge keeps 28
  • Recall@5 rose for 17–19 questions and fell for 11–17
Likely cause: domain mismatch. MiniLM was trained on web-search queries and bge on general data; our posts are long, informal forum posts and our questions come through ASR. Quality falls as N grows, and the larger model falls fastest.
Checked and ruled out: longer prompts (correlation with the score change 0.03 and 0.15) · truncation at 512 tokens (10–12% of pairs) · ASR errors (all runs share the transcripts) · judge noise (cannot explain the retrieval metrics)

Conclusion

What we can conclude

  • ASR: our kernels are correct (33 of 33 tests, 100% word accuracy), and the memory-bound ones are fast (RoPE at 80% of peak bandwidth). The simple tiled GEMM is 83% of GPU time and 7–10× below cuBLAS, so the model is 3.0× slower than PyTorch. Next: register tiling, and a KV cache.
  • RAG: off-the-shelf cross-encoders cost 32–647 ms per question, up to 1.6 GB of GPU memory and up to 21% longer prompts, with no measurable answer gain on this corpus. Better Recall@5 does help; these rerankers just do not rank this corpus better than enc_a.
  • Limitations: 125 dev questions; each answer judged once, by the model that wrote it, from the transcript; one GPU type and a shared ELM service.

Question 1

Our main design choice

What is one important decision you made when building or experimenting with your system, and why did you make it?
  • Reranking as a separate stage between search and prompt, with k fixed at 5 and the same cached transcripts in every run: only the choice and order of the 5 posts change
  • Why: 77% of relevant posts are in the top 100, but only 42% reach the top 5; answers score 0.14 without relevant evidence, 0.47 with it
  • Not chosen: a larger k (longer prompts, so two effects get mixed) · an approximate index (search takes 5 ms of a 9.2 s pipeline)

How we checked our work

How did you check that your system, or the part you worked on, was working as intended?
  • Unit tests with stub models: a fake cross-encoder makes the right order known in advance; one test checks that the baseline path is unchanged
  • Checks on the real runs: Recall@100 is 0.724 in every configuration, as it must be when only the top N is reordered; our sweep reproduces the supplied evaluate_rag.py baseline exactly
  • Example, testing the judge: 5 practice questions answered twice with identical inputs: 4 of 5 scores changed (mean 0.15, one by 0.5). So we compare paired means over 125 questions, with bootstrap intervals
